# Crit 10 reflection

The breakthrough that moved the work forward was making loading smoother. Little Worlds uses real-time 3D graphics, so entering the world is part of the experience, not just a step before playing. The Crit 10 work addressed arrival preparation and rendering load, and added clearer guidance about what to try if loading was slow or failed. My classmates also played, and I observed that the website ran normally. That gave me a concrete result to report, although it did not show that every possible action or remaining problem had been checked.

At the same stage, I also reflected on how I want to begin developing with AI. I want to start with a good idea, prepare a sample or reference that gives the AI something concrete to work from, and then begin implementation. I do not want to rush into building before the direction is clear. A reference helps communicate what I mean instead of leaving the AI to guess from an abstract description.

I want to be a developer who takes preparation as seriously as execution. My responsibility includes deciding what I am trying to make and choosing a useful example before asking the AI to build it. It also includes being precise about the result I have actually observed, rather than claiming more than that evidence supports.
