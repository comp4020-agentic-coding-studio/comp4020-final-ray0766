# Crit 9 reflection

> Written by me in Chinese; translated into English with AI assistance.

The decision that affected my work most this week came from a problem I found while playing the game myself. When my character walked close to a wall, the camera would suddenly swing up towards the sky, which got in the way of ordinary walking. I then asked the AI to try a series of changes, but some of them felt worse in actual play than what we had before. In the end I chose to go back to the earlier, better camera, keep the indoor view switch, and concentrate on the one problem of the camera tipping upwards near walls.

This changed my habit of handing every decision and every test to the AI. Automated tests can check whether the code meets the rules we set, but passing them does not mean the controls feel natural to a player. Whether the camera is comfortable, whether the character can be seen clearly, and whether a change really improves the experience are questions I still have to notice and judge from a person's point of view.

Afterwards I asked the AI explicitly to finish the necessary technical checks and then hand the work over, so that I could test the visuals and controls myself, instead of adding more tests and more changes. This experience reset how we divide the work: the AI implements, investigates and verifies the technical side; I play it myself and judge which problems are worth solving and which changes should stay.
