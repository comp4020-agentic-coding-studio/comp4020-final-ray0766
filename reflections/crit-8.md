# Crit 8 reflection

I started by looking for a project idea that felt original and could develop throughout the course. The first suggestions seemed too simple, so I asked the agents to research similar competitions and award-winning projects. [Messenger](https://messenger.abeto.co/)’s explorable small world caught my attention. I first considered reproducing its core experience, then chose a different direction: a universe where each player could claim a planet, build with shared modules, and fly to visit other players without changing their worlds. This gave building, persistence, and ownership rules a concrete purpose.

The breakthrough was turning that inspiration into a direction I could use to judge the work. The early low-poly appearance and tiny scale did not match the world I wanted, so I asked for a more convincing industrial harbour and resources of the same quality for player buildings. Separating Claude’s workshop work from the main system also clarified that an attractive demonstration did not prove its contents could be edited, saved, or used in the live project. For Crit 8, I prioritised a working release rather than adding those modules immediately.

The agents implemented the project and ran technical checks; I chose the direction, challenged results, and set priorities. I want to become a developer who can explain those choices and their limits. For the next stage, that means defining what a visitor should be able to do, what should still be there when they return, and what evidence would justify calling the work finished. It also means distinguishing local test results from a verified deployment.
