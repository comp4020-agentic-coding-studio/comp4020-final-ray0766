# Crit 8 reflection

> Prepared with AI assistance from my recorded decisions and the project evidence.

I started by looking for a project idea that felt original and offered enough depth to develop throughout the course. The first suggestions seemed too simple, so I asked the agents to research similar competitions and award-winning projects. Messenger's explorable small world caught my attention. I initially considered reproducing its core experience, then developed a different direction: a universe where each player could claim a planet, build it with shared modules, and fly to visit other players without changing their worlds. This turned the initial inspiration into a concrete purpose for building, persistence, and ownership rules.

I also kept questioning the gap between the intended experience and the result.
The early low-poly appearance and tiny scale did not match the world I was asking
for. I requested a more convincing industrial harbour and the same quality of
resources for player buildings. Separating Claude's workshop work from the main
system clarified another gap: an attractive demonstration does not establish that
its contents can be edited, saved, or used in the live project. For Crit 8, I chose
to prioritise a working release over adding those modules immediately.

The agents implemented and ran the technical checks; my role was to choose the
direction, challenge results, and set priorities. For the next stage, I want to
make that direction more precise: describe what a visitor should be able to do,
what must survive their return, and what evidence would justify calling it finished.
That means being able to explain the choices and their limits, including the
difference between local test evidence and a verified deployment.
